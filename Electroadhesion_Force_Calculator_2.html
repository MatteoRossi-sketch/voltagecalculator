About the model
  • Geometry. The electrodes are modelled by their cross-section: parallel strips of width w (edge to edge) separated by gaps s, alternating +V/2 and −V/2 on the plane z = 0. The pitch p = w + s is the centre-to-centre distance between neighbouring electrodes, and the period is L = 2p. Electrodes are infinitely thin. The real pad has the same section but a more complex electrode layout in plan. The model assumes that section holds everywhere over the grip area, and it does not include finger ends, bends or bus bars.
  • Stack. Above the electrodes: coating, then the gap between the electrode surface and the target, then the target (floating conductor, film of thickness t, or a dielectric halfspace). Below: a substrate halfspace.
  • Interface. The film does not lie at one gap. Over a fraction φ of the grip area it is in contact with the electrode surface and a nanometre-scale gap gc remains. Over the rest it rests on the roughness peaks, at a gap gr of micrometres. Each part is solved with the field model on its own and the pressure is the area-weighted mean, P = V² [ φ P₁(gc) + (1 − φ) P₁(gr) ], where P₁ is the pressure per V². This treats the two kinds of area as independent patches, each much larger than its own gap and much smaller than the pitch. φ is a bearing-area fraction: it can be measured from a profilometer trace, or from a contact test, and it is the one empirical input.
  • Solution. A per-harmonic admittance for the layered medium, a mixed boundary-value problem (potential fixed on fingers, charge free in the gaps) solved by Galerkin with ten edge-singular Chebyshev terms, and pressure from virtual work, P = −(ε₀/4) Σ φₙ² dYₙ/dg. Odd harmonics are summed to n = 480,001. The sum is cut off there, so gaps below about 5 nm read a few percent low (about 3 % at 1 nm).
  • Voltage. Linear electrostatics makes pressure exactly proportional to V², so the required voltage is solved in closed form.
  • Why not one gap. A single effective gap of 24 nm reproduced the first four bench tests, but manufactured electrodes are 3.2 to 10 µm rough, and at those gaps alone the field model needs 970 to 1,300 V/√g against the 480 to 510 V/√g measured. No area fraction fixes that, because it would have to exceed 1. The bench forces are 4 to 7 times larger than the roughness gap allows for the first four tests, and 2 to 4 times for the last two. Part of the film must be much closer to the electrodes than the roughness suggests. The two-level interface says so explicitly, and φ is how much.
  • Comparison with the bench. With gc = 1 nm and gr from 3.2 to 10 µm, tests 1 to 4 need φ = 0.59 to 0.73, and φ = 0.64 reproduces them within an rms error of 3 % in voltage. Tests 5 and 6 need φ = 0.21 to 0.30, and at φ = 0.64 the bench needs about 28 % more voltage than the model. Between 3.2 and 10 µm the answer for φ barely moves, which is why it can be quoted without knowing the roughness exactly. It does depend on gc: going from 1 to 2 nm raises it by about 0.04. The model does not explain why φ drops for the two heaviest payloads: more voltage should press the film harder onto the electrodes, not less. A load-dependent area, peeling at the payload edge or a change in how the film is held are candidates, and none is modelled here. The unipolar tests need 5 to 14 % more voltage than the bipolar ones, which the model cannot show because it sees only the voltage difference.
  • What φ can hide. φ absorbs anything that lowers the force relative to full contact: real contact area, an area that does not take part in the grip, and charge that adds to or subtracts from the field. A fitted φ is a calibration of the model against the bench, not a measured contact area, until it is checked against a profilometer or a contact test. A φ of 0.6 on a 50 µm film pressed by a few pascals is high, and that should be tested.
  • Single-harmonic comparison. Uses payload weight over the free gripping area, adds the film thickness to the coating depth, takes the coating permittivity, and uses the roughness gap with no contact. It is compared with the model at the entered φ.
